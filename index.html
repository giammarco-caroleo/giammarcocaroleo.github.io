Giammarco Caroleo

PhD Student — Engineering Science

Oxford Robotics Institute, University of Oxford

I develop distributed proximity and tactile perception strategies and sensor-driven control for manipulators, with a focus on safe, robust behaviour in cluttered and dynamic environments.

About

Soon-to-be Doctor of Engineering Science at the University of Oxford (Oxford Robotics Institute), I integrate sensing, perception and control for real robotic systems. My work spans hardware (miniaturized Time-of-Flight sensors and tactile skins integration), perception (scene flow, visuo-tactile embeddings, SLAM), and control (task-priority controllers, tactile/proximity-driven reactive policies).

My PhD is fully supported by an EPSRC DTP studentship. Prior experience includes industry traineeships at Siemens Industry Software NV (Leuven, Belgium) and a research fellowship at the University of Calabria (Cosenza, Italy) within the Mechanical Engineering Department. I enjoy connecting research to broader audiences through demos and outreach (see highlights below).

Research Highlights

Here's a list of selected projects; video demos on the left, short description and relative links on the right. For a full list of publications, please visit the Publications page.

Tiny LiDARs for Manipulator Self-Awareness

Caroleo, G., Albini, A., De Martini, D., Barfoot, T.D., Maiolino, P.

In this work we use miniaturized Time-of-Flight sensors mounted on a manipulator to localize objects in its surroundings. We present sensor characterization, a probabilistic sensor model, and initial localization experiments validating the approach.

Paper • YouTube demo

Estimating Scene Flow with Distributed Tiny LiDARs

Sander, J., Caroleo, G., Albini, A., Maiolino, P.

We use distributed miniaturized Time-of-Flight sensors to estimate scene flow in the robot’s surroundings, enabling fast motion inference from sparse, noisy range signals. This is useful for collision avoidance and responsive control in dynamic scenes.

Paper • YouTube demo

A Proxy–Tactile Reactive Control for Robots Moving in Clutter

Caroleo, G., Giovinazzo, F., Albini, A., Grella, F., Cannata, G., Maiolino, P.

This work introduces a proxy-tactile controller that lets robots move safely in clutter by reacting both to proximity and tactile feedback — fear no more, your robot can now navigate complex environments with confidence.

Paper • YouTube demo

Cross-Task Visuo–Tactile Representation using Point Clouds

Caroleo, G., Albini, A., Maiolino, P.

We present an augmented point-cloud representation that fuses vision and physical properties and can be used across tasks. A simple yet effective way to combine visual and tactile information in an interpretable unified representation.

Paper • YouTube demo
